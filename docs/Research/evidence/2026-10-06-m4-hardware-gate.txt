Finding from the first run: every codec and both paths composited ~2808 of 3600 frames at
1080p60 (~47 fps on a 60 Hz panel), so decode was not the limit. Per-stage timing added to
the audit (`M4 audit stages/UI/Qt frame` lines) showed composites of 3.5 ms (hardware) and
8 ms (software), Qt frames of 4.7 ms and UI ticks every 8 ms, but composites only every
~23 ms: the audio clock advanced only at PipeWire callbacks (about 21 ms of audio each), so
video following it jumped one or two frames per step. PipeWireOutput::frames_played now
interpolates between callbacks with the steady clock, capped at one quantum.

Decision under the predeclared gate: hardware decode passed every 1080p60 codec (H.264,
10-bit HEVC, AV1: 2 of 2 each), 2160p30 H.264 and the 10-minute 1080p30 run (drift 0.03 ms, no
drops), with stable memory; software failed 10-bit HEVC 1080p60 twice and H.264 1080p60 once.
Hardware decode becomes the viewer default on Intel (the tested driver); other vendors stay on
software until S7/S8. Seek-to-first-frame is slower with hardware for H.264 (p50 136 vs 88 ms)
and 2160p (208 vs 150 ms), faster for HEVC and AV1. Not covered: two-layer scenes, other
GOP structures, hybrid machines.
