5. Blur after the rework (same benchmark, release libs, two runs, medians). The source is
   linearized and box-reduced by a factor f = max(sigma / 3, source px per output px), the
   gaussian runs on the reduced image (radius <= 18), the composite reads it back bilinearly;
   intermediates RGBA16F (were RGBA32F at the full source size).

   case                      1080p source        2160p source (shown at 1080p)
   plain                     2.69-2.71 ms        4.75-4.84 ms
   blur 10% (sharpness -0.1) 7.87-8.28 ms        10.96-11.59 ms    (was 10.1-10.5 / 57-58)
   blur max (sharpness -1)   3.51-3.59 ms        14.38-14.72 ms    (was 52.6 / 209-214)
   sharpen max               8.53-8.58 ms        11.61-11.72 ms    (was 8.8-8.9 / 42-45)

   Intermediate steps, 2160p source: the reduction alone (RGBA32F) gave blur max 13.2-14.1 ms
   but left blur 10% at 45-52 ms and sharpen at 46 ms (factor 1, full-size float images);
   RGBA16F brought those to 36-38 and 29-30 ms; the display factor to the numbers above.
   Blur max at 2160p (f = 14) costs more than blur 10% (f = 2): each reduce thread averages
   196 decoded texels serially. Done afterwards: a separable reduce (across into a
   width-reduced image, then down), two runs, 2160p source: blur max 5.65-5.83 ms (plain
   4.73-5.65), blur 10% 12.0-12.5 ms, sharpen 12.0-12.3 ms; 1080p: blur max 2.80-3.13 ms.
   GPU vs CPU on the factor-2 test: max difference 2.5e-3 (the across image is half float).
   Left as is: with factor 1 the reduce-down pass is a plain copy (one full-size read and write);
   blur 10% at 1080p costs 8.5-10.2 ms against 2.7 ms plain.
   Strength: sigma = 2% of the source height per unit at any resolution (it was clamped to
   21.3 px, half the relative blur for a 2160p source).

   Tests: GPU vs CPU with a factor-2 blur and a crop on a 640x360 still: max difference 9.7e-4.
   With RGBA16F intermediates the looks test (blur + sharpen layers) rose from 2.3e-3 to
   4.5e-3 maximum, 0 of 230400 pixels over the 4e-3 tolerance.
